An independent project, not affiliated with the City of New Orleans. Every page links to the City dataset it uses. Source code: GitHub.

Notes on the data

General

  • Supporting data. Every page also uses the City's Neighborhood Statistical Areas (boundaries) and Centerline (the street map). Rates per resident or per home use Neighborhood Statistical Areas Demographics 2020, the City's table of 2020 Census population and housing units. The calls page also uses Police Districts.
  • Neighborhood groups. With a neighborhood picked, the filters list its neighborhood groups from the City's Self Reported Neighborhood Organizations, as the groups reported them: each group's name, linked to its website or Facebook page where it gave one, and when and where it meets. A group is listed in each neighborhood that holds a tenth of its area, or a quarter of whose area it covers; ten neighborhoods have none on the list. Contact people are left out, and so are email addresses unless the address is plainly the group's own (such as info@ on its website's domain) and meeting places given only as a street address, which may be someone's home. The list is only as current as the City's.
  • Population. Resident counts come from the 2020 Census, the most recent official count. The site will keep using them until the 2030 Census results are released, so population data is slightly out of date.
  • Every neighborhood is counted. Maps open on the populated city, so Lake Catherine and Viavant–Venetian Isles (under 100 residents per square mile) start out of view: zoom out or pick them from the menu.
  • Maps show a dot for each record that has a location inside a neighborhood. Records at the very same spot share one dot; its tooltip says how many more are there. The Heatmap checkbox shades each neighborhood by how many of the dots shown are in it; picking a neighborhood zooms to it but leaves the others shaded for comparison.
  • Shaded maps use up to seven shades with about the same number of neighborhoods in each (fewer when many neighborhoods share a value, such as zero); the key shows where each shade starts, rounded to a round number where that moves no more than one neighborhood into another shade.
  • Neighborhood figures are totals unless the key says per 1,000 residents, per 100 or 1,000 homes, or a share.
  • The vacant land page also uses the City's Parcels layer, only to place NORA's lots on the map.

City Planning Cases

  • Cases with several types are grouped under the most consequential one. Topics come from keywords in case descriptions. A neighborhood notice (NPP) and the case it leads to are separate records; NPP notices start in 2016, which raises yearly totals from then on. Rows the City's file repeats exactly are counted once. Hearing dates are the next meeting dates the City lists; a few fall on weekends or holidays and are flagged as listed. Agenda descriptions leave out the applicant's name.
  • The Zoning layer is the City's Zoning Districts, colored by broad group (the site's grouping of the City's district codes, not an official one); a district's tooltip gives its code and the City's description. Outlines are simplified to about 5 meters, so for a single lot, check the City Planning Commission's official zoning map. Overlay districts and conditional uses aren't shown.

311 Requests

  • The page starts with February 2019: the City's file dates about 313,000 older requests to January 1–2, 2019, when they were moved into its current system. Request types are the City's own; types it no longer uses are counted as Other. A request is "open" while its status is Pending, and "closed" when the City closed it in its system, which for some types means the work was assigned to a contractor or referred to another department, not that it was finished. Time to close counts requests closed in the past 12 months that have a close date; most closed abandoned-vehicle requests have none. Locations are where each request was reported; about 2% have none. The map has a dot for every request filed in the past 12 months and every request still open, however old; the City's file has no addresses, so a dot's tooltip gives its neighborhood. Requests can be about any place, so rates per 1,000 residents run high where few people live, like the CBD; neighborhoods with fewer than 1,000 residents get no rate.

Short-Term Rentals (STRs)

  • The map shows active licenses for commercial, non-commercial and exempt lodging rentals. Operator permits, which license the person running a rental rather than a place, are not mapped. "Per 100 homes" divides licenses by 2020 Census housing units; neighborhoods with fewer than 500 are left out. The City's license types changed with its rules in 2017, late 2019 and 2023, so the applications chart groups them into commercial, residential (accessory, residential owner and non-commercial), temporary and other. Outcomes are each application's current status; "since expired" means the license was issued and has expired. A few applications have no date and are left off the chart.

Blight/Code-enforcement

  • Yearly charts start in 2016: the City's records date about 76,000 cases and 59,000 hearings to 2009, when older records were moved into its system. Open cases are shown by the stage the City lists and by time since the case was filed. "New cases" are grouped by the result of the first inspection. Overgrown-lot cases are the City's grass-cutting (lot abatement) cases, which start in earnest in 2021. The abatement records show no property entering abatement since 2019, so the map shows only properties still in abatement; how closed abatement cases ended is counted for the whole city. Maps count cases with a location (all but about 1%), so their totals run slightly below the charts'. The map's dots are every open case, every case filed in the past 12 months, grass-cutting cases open or filed in the past 12 months, and properties in abatement; the abatement records the site reads carry no dates. Each dot's tooltip gives the property's address as the City lists it. "Per 1,000 homes" divides by 2020 Census housing units; neighborhoods with fewer than 500 are left out.
  • Fines. From the City's Adjudication Enforcement Fines and Fees, refreshed nightly. That dataset covers several programs; the chart keeps code enforcement fines (one-time and daily), the court, administrative, recording and lien costs added to those cases, and the cost of grass cutting, lot cleaning, demolition and other work the City did and billed to the owner. One Stop Shop fees (permits, zoning, HDLC, Vieux Carré, short-term rentals) and other programs are left out. Years are when each fee was entered. About 130,000 fees, nearly all from cases opened in 2009–2011 when older records were moved into the City's system, carry a placeholder date (January 1, 2000), so they're counted under "before 2012, or no date". The dataset marks each fee paid or not paid and says whether its case is open or closed. It doesn't say why a fee is unpaid: an unpaid fee may have been reduced, waived or written off, placed as a lien on the property, be under appeal, or still be owed. Most unpaid dollars are on closed cases. Amounts are as assessed, not what was collected.

Permits

  • Counts are permits issued, by the date the City issued them. The City's permit codes are grouped into nine types; interior demolition counts as renovation, and City demolitions count as demolitions. Every permit issued in the past 12 months is a dot at its address; a dot's tooltip gives the address and the City's own permit type. Days to permit run from the application date to the issue date. Construction values are left out because some are clearly entry errors. "Per 1,000 homes" divides by 2020 Census housing units; neighborhoods with fewer than 500 are left out.
  • The Historic districts layer is the City's Local Historic Districts, last updated February 2025. The City's newer "Local Historic District" dataset (hea7-kccz) lists the same 22 districts by name but publishes no boundaries through its API, and doesn't say which are full or partial control. "Full" and "partial control" are as the City labels them; the HDLC explains what each reviews. Outlines are simplified to about 3 meters, so a permit right on a district's edge may fall on either side. Landmarks are the City's HDLC Landmarks, designated or nominated, with the address, date and architect it lists.

Trees

  • The City's tree inventory as last updated on April 17, 2025, exported once and not refreshed daily.
  • Tree requests are 311 requests of the type "Tree Service", refreshed daily with the 311 data; requests without a location aren't mapped. "Open" means still listed as pending; days to close run from filing to the City's close date. "Dead or dying tree" became a request reason in April 2026.

Drainage

  • Drainage requests are 311 requests of the type "Drainage", plus the drainage reasons the City also filed under "Roads/Drainage" until 2024, since February 2019 and refreshed daily with the 311 data. Reasons are grouped; some, like street flooding, are no longer used. "Open" means still listed as pending; "closed" means closed in the City's system, which can mean referred or assigned rather than finished. Days to close run from filing to the City's close date. Requests without a location (about 1%) aren't mapped.
  • Catch basins are the City's inventory of storm drain inlets. The City's cleaning and inspection log is reloaded every night, but its newest dated entry is January 2, 2025, and logged work fell from about 19,000 entries in 2017 to about 2,000 in 2024. So the map shows the last cleaning recorded, not how long ago a basin was cleaned. "None in the log" means no dated cleaning since 2017 was logged for that basin; it may still have been cleaned, by the City or by others, such as the Sewerage & Water Board. The log uses its own location ids, so each entry is matched to the inventory basin within 5 meters of its location (94% are). About 38,000 log rows have no date or work type and are left out. "Share of its basins" shades each neighborhood by the share of its basins shown.

Streets

  • Street requests are 311 requests about the road surface, sidewalks, curbs and ramps, utility cuts, debris and streetlights, under the types "Roads and Streets" and "Streetlights" and the older types they replaced ("Roads/Drainage" until 2024, "Traffic Signals/Signs/Striping/Streetlights" until 2023), since February 2019 and refreshed daily with the 311 data. Drainage reasons are on the Drainage page; signs and striping are left out. Reasons are grouped. "Open" means still listed as pending; "closed" means closed in the City's system, which can mean referred or assigned rather than finished. Requests without a location (about 1%) aren't mapped. The City's 311 data has no usable street address for the site to show.
  • Road work comes from the City's two RoadWork project lists: the Department of Public Works' paving projects and the Sewerage & Water Board's sewer, water and drainage line work, most of it under the same projects. Both list a project block by block, as a point on each street segment, so a project is many dots; a block in both lists is one dot, shown in the earlier of its two phases (under construction, then planned, then complete). The phase and project details are as the City lists them and may lag the work on the street. A project's total cost is the whole project's, repeated on every block in the City's data, so it's shown once per project and never added up by block or neighborhood. Many planned projects have no dates yet ("TBD"); the Sewerage & Water Board list has no dates or costs. Program names are expanded from the City's codes (RR: FEMA-funded Recovery Roads; DPW: Public Works; SELA: Southeast Louisiana drainage). The lists' outreach contacts are not shown.
  • "City work within 60 m" looks for a road work block point within 60 meters (about half a block) of each road request; a request on a long block can be farther than that from its block's point. It shows whether the City lists work on that stretch of street, not whether the work will fix the request. Streetlight requests aren't matched. The City's streetlight inventory has no outage or condition data, so it isn't mapped.

Vacant Land

  • NORA's uncommitted lots are its Uncommitted Property Inventory: property the New Orleans Redevelopment Authority holds that is not under contract or committed to one of its programs. Being on the list does not mean a lot is for sale or open to any buyer; NORA sells through its own programs, described at noraworks.org. The list has no map locations, so each lot is placed inside its parcel, found by its geopin in the City's Parcels layer (all 214 were found).
  • Public land is the City's list of sites owned or managed by the City and other public agencies. It was last updated in November 2025, so some sites may have changed hands since: 420 of them have a NORA sale on record for the same parcel, almost all dated before that update. The map draws those faded, and can hide them or show only them. Types are the City's own, grouped for the map. Sizes are the City's acreage where given, otherwise measured from the site's outline. Each dot sits inside its site's outline; 4 of 2,149 sites have no usable outline in a neighborhood and aren't mapped. A NORA lot that is also on the City's list is shown once, as a NORA lot. Most of the City's notes are deed references and aren't shown; status notes such as "To be listed for sale" are.
  • NORA sales are every sale in NORA Sold Properties, by sale date, from 1980 to September 2026; only 18 are before 2000, where the yearly chart starts. How a lot was sold is NORA's own channel: Auction; Lot Next Door, its program for neighbors of a lot; Development; Alternative Land Use, its program for uses such as gardens and green space; and Other (five special transfers and one affordable-housing sale). The City's file has no buyers. Dots are the City's geocoded addresses; 11 sales without one are placed by parcel and 1 isn't mapped. Some parcels were sold more than once.

New Businesses

  • "New applications" are occupational license applications, counted by the date filed; that dataset starts in 2018. Sectors are the City's NAICS categories; many recent applications don't have one yet ("Not stated"). Outcomes are each application's current status: "approved or issued" includes licenses that have since expired, and some older applications are still listed as filed or in review. "Licensed now" counts businesses holding a license today, with sectors matched from their business type; event and street vendors are grouped separately. Licenses with no location in the city, like many vendors and taxis, are not mapped. On the map each application since 2018, and each business licensed now, is a dot; "licensed since" is the business start date the City lists, and a few list none. Tooltips give each business's address, and for businesses licensed now its name, except where the business may be home-based: applications marked home-based, and current licenses of a home-based license type, at an apartment, or at an address where a home-based business has applied, show neither. A business name that is only its owner's own name, like a sole proprietor's, is left out too (the site checks it against the license's owner name, which it never shows), so the dot is labeled by its license type instead. Some addresses, such as office buildings and mailing addresses, hold hundreds of licenses, so their dots sit on one spot.

Calls for service

  • "Started by officers" means self-initiated. Arrival time runs from call creation to the first officer's arrival, for calls from the public only. The current month is left off the monthly chart until it's complete. NOPD withholds locations for medical and mental-health calls. The neighborhood map can show calls per 1,000 residents, using 2020 Census population. Counts include calls the City closed as duplicate or void (about 6%). NOPD cautions against comparing its call data over time, since classifications and systems change.

Drone flights

  • Takeoff times appear to be UTC and are shown in Central time. Purposes are grouped from a free-text field; when it's blank, the activity summary is used. Locations are takeoff points, not flight paths; flights from the same spot share one dot.

Pages not in the menu

The police calls for service and drone flights pages are off the menu for now. They are still updated daily, and links to them still work.
