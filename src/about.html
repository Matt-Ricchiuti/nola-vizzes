About this site

Hand-built charts and maps of the City of New Orleans' open data, refreshed automatically every morning.

What this is

The City of New Orleans publishes hundreds of datasets at data.nola.gov. The portal's built-in charts make it hard to see what's in them. Each page here takes one dataset and explains it: what's in it, what changed recently, and where the data has gaps or quirks.

This is an independent project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or produced by the City of New Orleans or any of its departments. For official figures, go to the City's own datasets, linked at the top of every page.

How it's updated

Every morning, an automated job downloads the latest data from the City's public API, recomputes every chart, and republishes the site. The "Data pulled" date at the top of each page shows when that last happened. The data was last pulled {{PULLED}}.

If the City's data comes back empty, a column disappears, or counts suddenly drop, the job stops and the site keeps showing the last good version, rather than publishing something broken.

The street trees page is the exception. It's built from a one-time export of the City's tree inventory (October 2026) and isn't refreshed daily.

Caveats

  • Every page has an "About this data" section listing what we changed or grouped, such as combining free-text categories or converting time zones.
  • Neighborhood totals are counts, not rates per resident.
  • Records can be corrected or reclassified by the City after the fact, so a number on this site can change from one day to the next.

Source code

The code that downloads, processes and draws everything is public on GitHub. If you spot an error, you can open an issue there.
